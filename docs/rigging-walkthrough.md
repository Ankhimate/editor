# Rigging walkthrough

A rig is three things stacked: **bones** that move, **slots** that say what draws
and in what order, and **attachments** that are the actual art. Animation is a
fourth layer on top, and it only ever records *offsets* from the rigged pose.

The fastest way to understand it is to open a finished one.

## Open the sample

```bash
cargo run -p ankhimate-formats --example make_sample   # writes samples/walker.ankh
cargo run -p ankhimate-editor
```

Then **File ▸ Open** → `samples/walker.ankh`, or pick it from the startup window.

You get a 12-bone character with a 1-second walk cycle. Press **Tab** to switch
between Setup and Animate, and **Space** to play.

Everything in it — including the art — is generated by
[`formats/examples/make_sample.rs`](../formats/examples/make_sample.rs), which is
worth reading alongside this document: it is the same rig expressed as about a
hundred lines of code.

## What to look at first

**Press Tab twice.** Setup mode shows the rig as built; Animate shows it at the
playhead. Edits mean different things in each, and the editor refuses the wrong
one rather than silently corrupting the rig — that distinction is the single most
important thing about the tool.

**Select `thigh_l` in the Hierarchy** and rotate it in Setup. That changes the
character's build. Undo, press Tab, and rotate it again: now you are posing, and
the change becomes a key.

**Scrub the timeline.** The dopesheet rows are bone properties; the strip under
the ruler is the event lane, with two `footstep` markers at 0.0 and 0.5.

**Try the IK.** In Setup mode, select `foot_target_l` and drag it. The near
leg's thigh *and* shin both solve to reach it — that is IK. Rotating `thigh_l`
by hand and watching the shin trail along is FK, which is a different thing and
easy to mistake for the same one.

Then switch the clip to **`leg_ik`** and play. It keys the foot target only; the
leg works out its own angles. Compare its dopesheet with `walk`, which keys four
bones by hand to do a similar job.

The two clips share one rig because the constraint's mix is *keyed*: `walk` sets
it to 0 at frame 0 so the hand-authored FK plays untouched, while Setup and
`leg_ik` leave it at 1. A constraint is not all-or-nothing for the whole rig.

## Building one from scratch

1. **Import art.** Drag PNGs onto the canvas. Each one becomes an asset.
2. **Setup mode** (Tab). Everything below is rig structure.
3. **Draw bones.** Press `B`, then drag from where the joint belongs, along the
   limb. Drag from an existing bone to make a child — a limb is a chain, and the
   hierarchy is what makes a shoulder carry the whole arm.
4. **Make slots.** Select a bone, then **+** in the Hierarchy panel. A slot is
   the thing that draws; it hangs off exactly one bone.
5. **Attach art.** With the slot selected, pick an image in the Properties panel.
6. **Fix the pivots.** The Properties panel has a 9-dot grid: a limb should
   usually pivot at the end that stays put — the shoulder end of an upper arm,
   not its middle. This is the step that most often makes a rig look wrong when
   skipped.
7. **Order the draw.** The Draw Order panel is back-to-front. The far arm goes
   behind the torso, the near arm in front.

Now the rig exists. Press Tab.

8. **Make an animation** in the timeline panel, set its duration.
9. **Pose and key.** Move the playhead, rotate a bone, press `K`. Auto-key can
   do this for you; the keyed-value dots in the inspector show what is keyed at
   the playhead.
10. **Play** with Space.

## Beyond the basics

- **Meshes** — convert a region attachment to a mesh in the Properties panel,
  then trace its silhouette from the artwork's alpha. Meshes bend; rectangles
  do not.
- **Weights** — press `W` and paint which bones own which vertices, or use
  Auto-weight as a starting point and fix it up.
- **Skins** — the Skins panel. One skeleton, many outfits; several can be worn
  at once, first match winning.
- **Physics** — add it to a hair or tail bone and it will sway on its own. "Run
  in Setup" lets you tune the values without an animation to play.
- **Paths** — a curve bones follow, for tails, treads and belts.
- **Clipping** — a polygon that masks everything drawn after it.

## The two things people get wrong

**Pivots.** A bone rotates its art around the attachment's pivot, not around the
image's centre. An arm pivoting at its middle windmills instead of swinging.

**Which mode you are in.** A rotation in Setup rebuilds the character; the same
rotation in Animate is a key on the current clip. If an edit "did not stick",
check the mode indicator in the toolbar first.
